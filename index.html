      At the table

        House rules — Five Card 3/2 Draw
        • Deck: 54 cards — the standard 52 plus 2 jokers.
        • Wild: jokers and all deuces (2s) are wild — they can stand for any card.
        • Ante: 10 chips from everyone, every hand.
        • Draws: you draw twice — up to 3 cards after the first betting round, then up to 2 after the second.
        • Betting: three rounds — before the first draw, between the draws, and a final round after the second draw. Raises in 25 / 50 / 100 steps, max 3 raises a round.
        • Five of a kind is the best hand at this table — wild cards make it possible.

        House rules — Spades
        • Two ways to play (pick with the Spades toggle up top): 4P Teams — partners sit across, 13 cards each, first team to 500; 3P Cutthroat — everyone for themselves, the 2♣ sits out so it's 17 cards each, first to 300.
        • Bidding: everyone bids how many tricks they'll take, or Nil — a promise to take none (+100 if you pull it off, −100 if you don't). Partners' bids add up into one team contract.
        • Play: follow the led suit if you can. Highest card of the led suit takes the trick unless a spade lands — highest spade wins. Spades can't be led until they're broken (or they're all you have left).
        • Scoring: make your bid for 10 × bid + 1 per extra trick. Those extra tricks are bags — every 10 bags costs −100. Miss your bid and you're set: −10 × bid.
        • No chips in Spades — it's a points race. Your seat shows your match score.
        House rules — Blackjack
        • Pays: blackjack pays 3 to 2. Dealer stands on all 17s.
        • Double down on your first two cards — one card, then stand.
        • Seats play in turn: Don, then you, then Layton. The felt follows the turn.
        • Shoe: four decks, reshuffled when it runs low.
        House rules — Texas Hold'em
        • Blinds: 5 / 10 — small blind left of the button, big blind next. Heads-up, the button posts the small blind.
        • Hole cards: 2, private to you. Board: flop (3), turn (1), river (1), shared by everyone.
        • Betting: no-limit in 50 / 100 / 200 raise steps, max 3 raises a round — preflop, flop, turn, river.
        • Showdown: best five of your seven cards (2 hole + 5 board). The board itself can play — then the pot splits.
        • No wilds — a straight 52-card deck.
        House rules — Five Card Draw (classic)
        • Deck: a straight 52 — no jokers, no wilds; deuces are just deuces.
        • Ante: 10 chips from everyone, every hand.
        • One draw only — up to 5 cards after the first betting round, then a final betting round and showdown.
        • Betting: raises in 50 / 100 / 200 steps, max 3 raises a round.
        House rules — Farkle
        • Six dice. Roll, set aside at least one scoring die, then bank the turn total or roll what's left and press your luck. A roll with nothing scoring is a farkle — the turn's unbanked points are gone.
        • Every die you set aside has to score — one dead die in the pick makes the whole pick worth 0.
        • On the board: your first bank of the game must reach 500 in a single turn. Once you're on the board, any total banks.
        • Hot dice: set aside all six dice and they all come back — the turn total rides along.
        • Three farkles in a row cost 1,000 off your score (never below 0).
        • Winning: first to the target — 5,000 for a quick game, 10,000 for the full one (the host picks with the toggle up top). Crossing it opens a final round: everyone else gets one last turn to beat that total and steal the game.

        House rules — Uno
        • Deck: the standard 108 cards — in each color (Red, Yellow, Green, Blue): one 0, two each of 1–9, and two each of Skip, Reverse and Draw Two — plus 4 Wilds and 4 Wild Draw Fours. Seven cards each; the discard pile starts on a number card (action starters are buried and re-flipped).
        • Matching: play a card that matches the top card by color, number, or symbol — or any Wild. A Wild names the new active color.
        • Draw one: nothing to play (or you'd rather not)? Draw one card. If it plays, you may drop it right away; if it doesn't, your turn ends.
        • Skip — the next player loses their turn. Reverse — play flips direction (heads-up, it plays like a Skip). Draw Two — the next player draws 2 and loses their turn. Wild Draw Four — the next player draws 4, loses their turn, and you name the color.
        • No stacking, no challenges at this table — a Draw Two can't be answered with another Draw Two, and a Wild Draw Four is never challenged.
        • UNO! — play down to one card and the table calls it for you. No call-out penalties among friends.
        • Going out: play your last card and you score everything left in everyone else's hands — numbers at face value, Skip / Reverse / Draw Two at 20, Wilds at 50. If your last card is a Draw Two or Wild Draw Four, the next player still draws before the count.
        • No chips in Uno — your seat shows your session points; the room list keeps your games and score.
        House rules — Cards Against the Table
        • The game: a fill-in-the-blank party game with an original house deck — every card at this table was written for this room. Three or four players; empty seats fill with AI so the jokes never wait.
        • The Czar: each round one player is the Card Czar (purple badge) — the role rotates around the table every round. The Czar reads the black card and judges; the Czar does not play a card that round. (When the House deals, the host never czars.)
        • The black card has one blank — or two. Everyone else plays one white card per blank from their hand of seven; for a two-blank card, the order you pick them in is the order they read. You draw back up to seven after playing.
        • Anonymous: plays go in face-down and are shuffled before the reveal — nobody, not even the Czar, can see whose card is whose until the winner is crowned.
        • Scoring: the Czar crowns the funniest play; its author scores 1 point. First to 5 points wins the game. Ninety seconds to play your card(s), ninety for the Czar to pick — run out of time and the table picks for you.
        • No chips here — your seat shows your points; the room list keeps your games and score.
        House rules — Pool (8-Ball)
        • One-on-one. Fifteen balls and a cue ball, racked with the 8 in the middle. The break is taken from behind the head string, and the breaker alternates game to game.
        • Open table: groups aren't set until someone pockets a ball after the break. A ball on the break just stays down and the breaker shoots on — it doesn't claim solids or stripes.
        • Solids are 1–7, stripes 9–15. Pocket one of yours and you shoot again; pocket only your opponent's (or nothing) and the turn passes — their ball stays down either way.
        • Fouls: scratch the cue ball, or don't touch any ball at all, and your opponent gets ball in hand anywhere — even after the break. That's this table's one big simplification: no kitchen-only ball in hand.
        • The 8 ball: sink it before your group is cleared and you lose. Sink it on the break and it's a re-rack with the same breaker — no win, no loss. Clear your group, then sink the 8 to win — but scratch on the 8 and you lose.
        • No call shots at this table — slop counts, and the 8 doesn't need calling either. No spin in this first version: aim and power are the whole shot. Every screen replays the exact same physics from the shot's angle and power, so what the shooter saw is what everybody sees.
        • Aiming: drag on the table to aim (the guide shows first contact and where both balls roll — it shows only on the shooter's screen), set power with the slider, tap SHOOT. On a phone, landscape gives you a bigger table.
        • No chips in Pool — your seat shows your group and what's left; the room list keeps your games won.
